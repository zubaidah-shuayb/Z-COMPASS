The Story Behind Z-COMPASS

I think my journey with technology started earlier than I realised.

Back in junior secondary school—JS3, to be specific—I developed a serious interest in coding. Something about it immediately caught my attention. I was fascinated by the idea that you could sit in front of a computer, write something, and create something that did not exist before.

From that moment, I wanted to learn more.

So I did.

I studied whatever I could find. I watched, read, practised, experimented, and kept going. The more I learned about software development, the more interesting it became to me. At some point, I realised that this wasn't just something I found interesting.

I genuinely loved it.

Around 2022 to 2023, when I was just 14 years old, I enrolled in courses on Coursera and paid for my own learning. I wanted to study software development properly, and I was determined to keep going.

On March 12, 2023, I completed my IBM Full Stack Developer certification.

I was so excited.

Until I realised there was a problem.

I was not yet old enough to collect the certificate. 😭

It was disappointing, of course. I had worked for it, completed it, and was excited to finally have it. But I didn't see it as the end of anything.

I simply made myself a promise:

The day I turn 18, I will come back for my certificate.

So I kept learning.

And honestly, I didn't have the perfect setup either.

I was building and learning with a Dell computer that, at some point, was basically surviving by grace. 😂

It had been passed down through generations—allegedly.

Some keys were missing. A part of the screen had ink damage. There were times when I had to wait for electricity before I could even use it properly.

It was far from the dream setup.

But somehow, that didn't stop me.

If anything, it made me even more determined.

I kept learning.

I kept building.

I kept pushing.

Then came university.

I was 16 years old, and I already knew what I wanted. From the beginning, I had told my family that I wanted to study Computer Science.

But my family had a different idea.

They didn't really understand technology the way my generation does. My mum genuinely believed that if I studied Computer Science, I might end up hacking a governor's bank. 😭

And she meant it.

My family wanted me to study Medicine instead.

There was a lot of pressure.

I did well in my exams. I scored 323 in JAMB and had a high aggregate in my Post-UTME. Eventually, I found myself facing a decision that was much harder than any exam I had written.

I didn't know what to do.

I didn't know who to talk to.

And honestly, I felt like everyone around me had advice for me, but none of them truly understood what I wanted.

That period was difficult for me.

I felt confused. I felt frustrated. I felt like I was being pulled in a direction while a completely different part of me was still holding onto something I loved.

And that experience stayed with me.

It taught me something.

Sometimes, the hardest decisions in life aren't difficult because there are only two options.

They're difficult because there are too many voices.

Too many opinions.

Too much advice.

Too much pressure.

And somewhere in the middle of all of that noise, it can become hard to hear yourself.

During that period, I made a promise to myself.

One day, I would use technology to help people find their own direction.

Not to tell them what to do.

Not to make decisions for them.

But to help them slow down, understand what they were feeling, look at their situation from different angles, and figure out what truly mattered to them.

That promise eventually became the idea behind Z-COMPASS.

At first, I thought about building a decision-making tool that compared options, scored them, and helped users find the option with the highest alignment.

But as I developed the idea, I realised something important.

Life is not always a spreadsheet.

Relationships cannot always be reduced to percentages.

Career decisions cannot always be solved by giving one option a score of 90% and another 20%.

Sometimes, before someone can choose a direction, they need to understand what is actually happening inside the mess.

They need to ask themselves questions.

They need to notice patterns.

They need to understand their fears, priorities, hopes, and conflicts.

And that changed the entire direction of Z-COMPASS.

I stopped asking:

“How can AI tell someone the best decision?”

And started asking:

“What if AI could help someone understand their situation before trying to give them an answer?”

That became the heart of Z-COMPASS.

A compass does not choose your destination.

It does not walk the journey for you.

It does not force you to go in a particular direction.

But when you're lost, it can help you understand where you are and find your bearings.

That is what I want Z-COMPASS to be.

A space where someone can come with messy thoughts.

With fear.

With uncertainty.

With conflicting emotions.

With a situation they don't even know how to explain properly.

And instead of immediately being told what to do, they can hear:

“Okay. I hear you. Let's figure this out together.”

Today, in September 2026, I am 18 years old.

I am studying Medicine at university while continuing to build my career in technology.

I eventually got my IBM Full Stack Developer certificate—the one I promised myself I would collect when I turned 18. 🥹

I am still learning.

I am still building.

And I am currently learning penetration testing too.

Sometimes, when I think about how far I've come, I remember that young girl sitting in front of a struggling Dell computer, trying to learn how to code with missing keys and a damaged screen.

She had no perfect setup.

No perfect path.

And honestly, she didn't have all the answers.

But she kept going.

I think Z-COMPASS carries a little piece of that journey.

It came from a time when I felt confused, unheard, and surrounded by advice that didn't feel right for me.

It came from knowing what it feels like to have so many thoughts happening at once that you don't even know where to begin.

And it came from a simple belief:

People don't always need someone else to make their decisions for them.

Sometimes, they just need a little help finding their own way through the noise.

That is why I built Z-COMPASS.

You bring the mess.

Let's find your direction.
